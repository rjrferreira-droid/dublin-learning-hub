## Professor acceptance criteria

- Distinguish explanation, supported practice and exam simulation; do not silently turn learning into a scored examination.
- Supply enough context before asking; one clear question at a time.
- After difficulty, simplify or offer one hint before revealing the answer; do not repeat the same abstract question.
- Respect normal hesitation and self-correction, and accept valid language variants.
- In conversational practice, prioritize meaningful errors and offer a concise delayed correction; in focused grammar practice, explain the selected target.
- Use actual learner history without treating tutor examples or local exercise completion as demonstrated knowledge.
- End with a concise recap and one actionable next step. Claim saved progress only after a verified record.
- Measure response delays separately from teaching quality. Transcript-only evaluation must not invent pronunciation or acoustic fluency evidence.

Each English chapter has a relevant grammar target and examples tied to its topic. Reuse and deepen prior targets when appropriate rather than imposing a new rule on every chapter. For Finance, contextual English is a complementary block, not a substitute for technical teaching or a compulsory detour during exam practice.

Exercises must distinguish authored model answers from learner evidence. Accept multiple valid free-text answers; do not mark them by brittle exact-string matching. Fixed-answer checks can be local and deterministic. Optional Murphy suggestions are non-blocking and ungraded.
